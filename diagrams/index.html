How it fits together

The whole system, one script action on its way to the motor, and the access tiers. The numbers come from the spec and the firmware as of October 2026.

The system

The planner keeps a 128 ms strip of positions, one per millisecond. A planner stall shorter than that does not reach the motor. Below it, the low-power core gets a speed and a 4 ms lease every millisecond. If the main cores hang, the motor keeps its last speed for at most 4 ms, then stops.

How a funscript action reaches the motor

The machine picks how far ahead it accepts motion: 250, 500 or 1000 ms, and it ships at 1000. Phosphor sends each segment up to that horizon minus 100 ms ahead, so 900 ms by default. A Wi-Fi stall shorter than that doesn't reach the stroke, because the machine already holds the next stretch.

V8 and V9, the oscillation axes, take a separate path. Phosphor resamples them at the granted rate and sends them about 176 to 226 ms ahead on their own stream, and the planner adds them on top of the stroke.

Access tiers and pairing

Anyone can watch, and anyone connected can stop the machine. Moving it takes a pairing. The page the machine serves from its own address gets control through a one-time token, never configure. A factory-fresh machine gives configure to the first client that asks, since that is normally the owner setting it up.

PressOpens a 120 s pairing window. The first client to ask gets in.spec · details draft (RFC-114)

Hold 5 sDrops every paired client to watch and ends their sessions. Pairings stay on file.draft (RFC-114)

Power-cycle three times (no button)Three boots in a row, each shorter than 10 s, open the window on the next boot.spec (§12.3)

Draft gestures are in the firmware and can change before the spec adopts them. The spec also asks for a factory-reset gesture, which is not built yet.
