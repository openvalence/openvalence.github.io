OSSM Flagship board

The OSSM Flagship is a 58 x 58 mm controller board. It takes a 36 V supply, powers and commands a servo drive, and talks to the outside world through an ESP32-P4 with a stacked ESP32-C6. It runs Nucleus. The design is under CERN-OHL-S-2.0.

Status WIP

In design. The repo holds the KiCad 10 project, the parts list, the stackup and a dated ledger of design decisions. No fabrication files are published and no boards are for sale. Details can still change, and where FEATURES.md and SPEC.md disagree, SPEC.md wins.

Drive interface

The target is the YZ 60AIM40F integrated servo (200 W). The board is also built to drive the 350 W 60AIM40. It talks to the drive three ways:

Quadrature
A and B come from the P4's low-power core, buffered to 5 V, into the drive's opto inputs. So the drive follows the motion with no extra converter.
RS485 Modbus
Used for reads such as position. The board also supplies the drive's 485-side 5 V. So one cable carries signal and power for the drive's comms side.
Alarm and ready inputs
The drive's alarm line and ready line go to the P4. So a drive fault reaches the firmware directly.

Power and protection

  • 36 V bus in, 10 A continuous to the motor, 15 A fuse as the last-resort backstop. So a dead short blows a fuse instead of the board.
  • Surge stopper with reverse-polarity and reverse-current blocking. So regen from a motor coasting down never reaches the power supply or trips its overvoltage latch.
  • Regen clamp on the board with a port for an external brake resistor. It switches on at 44.5 V and off at 43.2 V, in hardware, with no firmware involved. So regen is handled even if the firmware is not running.
  • Motor switch with pre-charge and latching overcurrent trips, and a power monitor on a four-terminal shunt that reports bus voltage, current, power and energy. So firmware can see the load and back off before anything trips.
  • External E-stop input. Pressing the button, or unplugging it, cuts motor power in hardware. So a pulled E-stop cable stops the machine too.

On the board

  • M5Stack Stamp-P4 (ESP32-P4) with a Stamp-AddOn C6 stacked on top for Wi-Fi and BLE.
  • HOME and PAIR buttons, one status LED. No reset button.
  • Fan header with speed control and tach, and a thermistor input.
  • Optional accessory outputs: a fused 12 V pump output with a PWM signal, an external PWM output, a Neopixel chain, a Qwiic port and a GPIO header. Anything smarter than a switch belongs on its own board, over Valence (see Isotope).
  • 6 layers, parts on the top side only, 40 x 40 mm heatsink on the back. Three XT30 power connectors and JST GH for signals. So the heatsink sits under the power parts and the board stays small.

How it fits

The board is the physical end of the system. Nucleus is its firmware, and Valence is the protocol its hub speaks. The diagrams show the connections, and Components lists every part. The repo also reserves folders for cable drawings, enclosure parts and bench fixtures; they are empty so far.
