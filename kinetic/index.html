Kinetic

Kinetic is a header-only C++ library that plans jerk-limited trajectories. It has no hardware dependencies and its output is deterministic. It is the planner in Nucleus, and you can use it on its own. Apache-2.0.

Open the playground · Demos · Repo

Status

This page covers Kinetic², the current planner. It plans every motion in Nucleus: the firmware, the Virtual and the native tests. There is no release yet. Kinetic 1, the first planner, lives on the kinetic1 branch of the repo and is no longer on main.

The model

Knots in, a sampled trajectory out
Every source becomes knots: the curve passes position p at time t_us, optionally with an end velocity. The engine never sees a wire format. So any source, from a script to a live stream, feeds the same planner.
Event-driven
The pending knots are solved when they change, at the next sample. The curve is continuous in position and velocity by construction. So the motor never gets a step in speed.
Time never gives, amplitude does
A knot the limits cannot reach in time is moved toward the previous knot. It is never dropped and never placed late. So a script keeps its timing and gives up stroke length, not rhythm.
Limits are ceilings
The planner stays under velocity, acceleration and jerk limits and does not plan to reach them. Every trim, clamp and refusal is recorded as an anomaly you can read. So you can see exactly where a pattern asked for too much.

Free knots take one of two styles: crisp (no overshoot between monotone knots) or smooth (may overshoot, continuous acceleration), or a blend between. So you choose between exact turnarounds and softer curves.

Deterministic

Quantities are float; time is uint64_t microseconds that you supply. The same calls give bit-identical results on every IEEE-754 target, as long as everything that compiles the planner uses -ffp-contract=off and not -ffast-math. Without the flag, GCC fuses multiply-adds on targets such as the ESP32-P4 and the results drift by a few ULPs from native and WebAssembly builds. The test suite pins a fingerprint of the rendered motion. So the plan you preview in the browser is the plan the firmware runs, provided the flag is set.

Using it

Position is normalized 0 to 1 across the stroke window you give it, so mapping to millimeters, steps or encoder counts is your code's job. So the same plan works for any machine. You call submit() with knots and positionAt() on your own clock. It needs C++20 and builds with CMake or PlatformIO. A WebAssembly build with a small C interface is in the repo.

Kinetic plans motion inside a window. Homing, rail limits, e-stop, arbitration and step generation belong to the machine around it, and in OpenValence that is Nucleus.

Playground

The playground runs the planner, compiled to WebAssembly, in your browser. Place knots on the position trace, change the planner options, and read the result: the curve, velocity, acceleration and jerk against the limits, the solved knots and every anomaly. More in Demos.

How it fits

Nucleus pins Kinetic by commit and its lint fails if the checkout drifts from the pin. Valence segments become knots in the firmware's motion arbiter. The diagrams show where the planner sits, and Components lists the other parts.
