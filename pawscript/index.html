Pawscript, the free funscript generator

A free funscript generator for Windows. It tracks the motion in your video and writes the funscript automatically, with multi-axis lanes, a problem finder and playback on Buttplug toys and Valence hubs.

One Svelte 5 and Tauri 2 app with a small Rust backend and a Python tracker. Machine learning tracking models run on your graphics card, follow the motion in the video and write the script. Free software under GPL-3.0-or-later. No telemetry: the app goes online only when you download models or check for an update.

Made for explicit content. For adults only (18+).

Status

Early access, Windows only. Builds are on Downloads.

Features

  • Automatic tracking

    Paint the parts on one frame and TAPNext++ or SAM 2.1 follows them through the shot. The run is your script as soon as it lands. On an RTX 4090 at 1080p, TAPNext++ tracks about 25 frames a second. The seeder learns the parts from what you paint and suggests them on new frames once it has at least 5 painted examples of a part from 2 videos.

    Tracking guide

  • Lanes and axes

    Stroke plus roll, pitch, twist, surge, sway, vib and more, each on its own lane and written as its own funscript. About 50 transform tools shape amplitude, time, density, speed, rhythm and variation, each with a preview before it applies. Blender and OpenFunscripter keymaps.

    Editing guide

  • Review

    The problem finder checks the script against a device profile for 16 kinds of problem, such as overspeed, points too close and long gaps, and fixes 13 of them in one step. It reruns about 140 ms after each edit. The machine preview moves the way the device will.

    Review guide

  • Devices

    A Buttplug server inside the app drives toys over Bluetooth LE, serial and HID, in sync with the video. On a Valence hub such as Nucleus or Neutrino, Pawscript sends timed segments at least 100 ms ahead and the hub plans the motion within its own limits. The panic stop works from anywhere in the app.

    Device playback guide

More features

  • Scenes and cuts

    Finds hard cuts, fades, dissolves and still frames. You add, merge and move cuts.

  • Scene tags

    A shot tagger tags each scene.

  • Loops

    The loop finder marks exact loops in a scene.

  • Continuous projects

    The project writes itself about a second after each change.

  • Commit and publish

    Commits with notes. Publish writes numbered funscripts beside the video.

  • Export

    All axes, one axis or a device-safe copy.

  • Packs

    One .pawpack file holds the video, the scripts and the project.

  • Tabs

    Several projects at once. A track keeps running in a hidden tab.

  • Node graph

    Wire axes and tracker runs through nodes. The document changes when you bake.

  • Statistics and compare

    Speeds and strokes per minute by chapter, and a diff against a reference funscript.

  • Video formats

    mp4, m4v, mkv, webm, mov and avi.

  • Themes

    Dark, light and high contrast.

System requirements

  • Windows

    Windows 10 or 11, 64-bit, about 15 GB free. A portable folder on a local drive, outside OneDrive and other synced folders.

  • NVIDIA

    GTX 16xx or RTX 20xx and newer, driver 580 or newer, PyTorch on CUDA 13.0. The default settings use about 4 GB of video memory.

  • AMD

    Radeon RX 9000 and RX 7000, and the Radeon 780M and 760M, on a PyTorch ROCm runtime. Adrenalin 26.11.2 or newer.

  • Without a supported card

    The editor, playback and the CPU trackers work. TAPNext++, SAM 2.1 and the shot tagger need the card.

Download

Get Pawscript.zip from the latest release on GitHub. This site never hosts binaries. Extract it into a folder of its own and start Pawscript.exe; the first start downloads the runtime (2.3 GB) and the models you pick.

The exe is not code-signed. If SmartScreen stops it, click More info, then Run anyway.

Verify: the runtime and the tracking models come from dl.openvalence.org, and the app checks each file against the SHA-256 in the manifest built into it.
