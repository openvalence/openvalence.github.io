Try it without a machine

The planner runs in your browser. Phosphor includes Neutrino, an emulated machine that runs on your desktop. Neither needs a PCB.

Kinetic playground

The same Kinetic² planner that runs in the firmware, compiled to WebAssembly. Click the position trace to drop a knot, tune the planner options, and see what it did: the curve, its speed, acceleration and jerk against the ceilings, and every trim it had to make.

Kinetic gives bit-identical results on every target, so with the same knots and settings, the curve in your browser is the curve the machine plans.

Open the playground · Open the handles editor · About Kinetic

The handles editor loads a funscript as a chain of Bézier curves. Drag a knot's handles to set how fast it passes through. Bring your own .funscript or use the built-in one.

Phosphor and Neutrino

Neutrino is the machine's firmware compiled to WebAssembly, running inside Phosphor. It uses the same Valence protocol as a real machine, so the clip shows the real protocol, catalog and planner, without a motor.

It comes with Phosphor: connect to "Neutrino" in the machine list. Pre-release builds are on Downloads, and the Neutrino page explains how it works.
